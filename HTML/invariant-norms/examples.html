Examples of invariant norms (in alphabetical order)

Autonomous norm. Let G be a Frechet-Lie group; this includes real Lie groups and diffeomorphism groups. Let $S={\rm exp}(\mathfrak g)$ be the image of the Lie algebra of G with respect to the exponential map. The word norm associated with the set S is called the autonomous norm on G.

Facts:

  • If G is a real Lie group then its autonomous norm has finite diameter.
  • If G is the group of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of a closed surface then the autonomous norm is strongly unbounded.
  • The diameter of the autonomous norm on ${\rm Ham^c}(\mathbf R^{2n})$ is bounded by 3.

Commutator length. The commutator subgroup [G, G]⊴G of a group G is a normal subgroup generated by commutators [g, h], where g, h ∈ G. The word norm on [G, G] associated with this generating set is invariant under conjugation by elements of the group G. It is called the commutator length. In particular, if G is perfect (i.e., G = [G, G]) then the commutator length is a conjugation invariant norm on G.

Fact. If G admits an unbounded quasimorphism then the commutator length on [G, G] is stably unbounded.

Covering number. If G is generated by a conjugacy class C ⊆ G then the associated word norm is called the covering number of G (notice the issue with the symmetry in this definition). This invariant has been extensively studied for finite groups. Especially, many groups with covering number equal to 2 have been identified.

Warning: The covering number of a group G also means the smallest number of proper subgroups of G whose union is equal to G.

Hofer's norm. Let (M, ω) be a symplectic manifold and let ${\rm Ham}(M,\omega)$ be the group of its Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms. By definition every Hamiltonian diffeomorphism f if the time-one map of a time-dependent flow ft associated with a vector field Xt defined by ιXtω = dHt, where $H\colon M\times \mathbf\to \mathbf R$ is a function. The length of the isotopy {ft} is defined by
$$ \mathcal L (f_t) = \int_0^1 {\rm osc}F_t\ dt. $$
The Hofer norm of $f\in {\rm Ham}(M,\omega)$ is defined to be the infimum of the lengths of all isotopies from the identity to f:
$$ \nu_{\rm Hofer}(f) = \inf\ \{\mathcal L(f_t)\ |\ f_0=1\ \text{ and } f_1 = f\ \}. $$
It is easy to show that this formula defines a pseudonorm on ${\rm Ham}(M,\omega)$. The fact that it is nondegenerate is nontrivial.

Remark. Replacing the L∞ oscillation norm in the definition of the length of an isotopy by an Lp norm results in the degeneration of the modified Hofer norm.

Palindromic length. Let S ⊆ G be a symmetric subset generating G. An element g ∈ G is called a palindrome if g = s1⋯sn = sn⋯s1 for some si ∈ S. The palindromic length is the word norm associated with the generating set consisting of all palindromes. A conjugate of a palindrome is not a palindrome in general but it is a product of two palindromes. That is why the palindromic length is a conjugation invariant norm.

Verbal norm. Let $w\in \mathbf F_n$ be a reduced word in the free group of rank n. Let S = {s ∈ G | s = ψ(w±1)}, where $\psi\colon \mathbf F_n\to G$ is a homomorphism. The word norm on the group GS generated by S is called the verbal norm. It is invariant with respect to all automorphisms of G preserving GS. In particular, if GS = G then the verbal norm is Aut(G)-invariant.

Volume of the support. Let $f\in {\rm Diff}(M,{\rm vol})$ be a volume preserving diffeomorphism the volume of the support of f defines a norm vol-supp on the group ${\rm Diff}(M,{\rm vol})$.

Facts:

  • The vol-supp norm is non-discrete; indeed, there are diffeomorphisms of supported on sets of arbitrarily small positive volume.
  • The cyclic subgroup ⟨f⟩ is bounded by vol-supp(f).
  • If M is of infinite volume then the diameter of vol-supp is infinite.

The word norm. If G is generated by finitely many conjugacy classes then the Lipschitz class of the associated word norm is maximal among all conjugation invariant norms on G (see Basic Facts). It is called the word norm on G.
