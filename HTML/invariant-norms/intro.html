Introduction

An invariant norm on a group G is a nonnegative function which is symmetric, satisfies the triangle inequality and is invariant with respect to certain automorphisms of G. The most important class consists of norm invariant under conjugation. Although such norms have been studied - often under different names - for a long time, the first systematic treatment was done by Burago, Ivanov and Polterovich in [arxiv:0710.1412], where the basic notions were abstracted and a number of stimulating open problems asked.

Most of the invariant norms are word norms with a few notable exceptions. Let us discuss some areas of mathematics where invariant norms are present.

  • Symplectic geometry. The group ${\rm Ham}(M,\omega)$ of compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of a symplectic manifold (M, ω) admits a natural conjugation invariant norm called the Hofer norm. This norm is induces a nondiscrete and separable topology. This is highly unusual and it is one of the wonders of symplectic topology, according to [Polterovich-Rosen-2014].

  • Dynamics. Groups of diffeomorphisms of manifolds have geometrically meaningful generators; e.g., elements included in flows of vector fields, conjugacy classes with prescribed dynamical features, elements supported in sets with prescribed properties. The associated word norms for such generating sets are conjugation invariant.

  • Finite groups. Finite simple groups admit interesting conjugation invariant generatings sets; e.g., single conjugacy class, sets of elements of a given order. The associated (conjugation invariant) word norms have been extensively studied. Of course, every norm on a finite group has finite diameter. However, it is interesting to study norms suitably defined on infinte families of finite groups adnd to obtain uniform bounds. For example, there exists a constant c > 0 such that any element in any finite simple group is a product of at most c involutions [Liebeck-Shalev-2001].

  • Metric geometry. The fundamental group of a metric space is its homotopy invariant. However, it can be equipped with a geometrically defined conjugation invariant generating set and hence the associated word norm will reflect the metric structure of the space.
