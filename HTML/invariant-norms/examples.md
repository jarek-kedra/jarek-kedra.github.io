### Examples of invariant norms (in alphabetical order) ###


<a href="autonomous">
</a>
**Autonomous norm.** Let $G$ be a Frechet-Lie group; this includes real Lie
groups and diffeomorphism groups. Let $S={\rm exp}(\mathfrak g)$ be the
image of the Lie algebra of $G$ with respect to the exponential map.
The word norm associated with the set $S$ is called the *autonomous norm*
on $G$. 

_**Facts:**_ 

* If $G$ is a real Lie group then its autonomous norm has
finite diameter. 
* If $G$ is the group of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms
of a closed surface then the autonomous norm is strongly unbounded.
* The diameter of the autonomous norm on ${\rm Ham^c}(\mathbf R^{2n})$
is bounded by $3$.


-------

<a href="commutator-length">
</a>
**Commutator length.** The commutator subgroup $[G,G]\trianglelefteq G$ of a
group $G$ is a normal subgroup generated by commutators $[g,h]$, where 
$g,h\in G$. The word norm on $[G,G]$ associated with this generating set is
invariant under conjugation by elements of the group $G$. It is called the
*commutator length*. In particular, if $G$ is perfect (i.e., $G=[G,G]$) then the
commutator length is a conjugation invariant norm on $G$.

_**Fact.** If $G$ admits an unbounded quasimorphism then the commutator
length on $[G,G]$ is stably unbounded._ \


-------


<a href="covering-number">
</a>
**Covering number.** If $G$ is generated by a conjugacy class $C\subseteq G$
then the associated word norm is called the *covering number* of $G$ (notice
the issue with the symmetry in this definition). This invariant has been
extensively studied for finite groups. Especially, many groups with
covering number equal to $2$ have been identified.

*Warning:* The covering number of a group $G$ also means the smallest number
of proper subgroups of $G$ whose union is equal to $G$. \


----------

<a href="hofer-norm">
</a>
**Hofer's norm.** Let $(M,\omega)$ be a symplectic manifold and let ${\rm
Ham}(M,\omega)$ be the group of its Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms.  By definition
every Hamiltonian diffeomorphism $f$ if the time-one map of a time-dependent flow
$f_t$ associated with a vector field $X_t$ defined by 
$\iota_{X_t}\omega = dH_t$, 
where $H\colon M\times \mathbf\to \mathbf R$ is a function.
The *length* of the isotopy $\{f_t\}$ is defined by
$$
\mathcal L (f_t) = \int_0^1 {\rm osc}F_t\ dt.
$$
The *Hofer norm* of $f\in {\rm Ham}(M,\omega)$ is defined to be the infimum
of the lengths of all isotopies from the identity to $f$:
$$
\nu_{\rm Hofer}(f) = 
\inf\ \{\mathcal L(f_t)\ |\ f_0=1\ \text{ and } f_1 = f\ \}.
$$
It is easy to show that this formula defines a pseudonorm on
${\rm Ham}(M,\omega)$. The fact that it is nondegenerate is nontrivial.

*Remark.* Replacing the $L^{\infty}$ oscillation norm in the
definition of the length of an isotopy by an $L^p$ norm results in the degeneration of the
modified Hofer norm. 

---------

<a href="palindromic-length">
</a>
**Palindromic length.** Let $S\subseteq G$ be a symmetric subset generating $G$.
An element $g\in G$ is called a *palindrome* if 
$g = s_1 \cdots s_n = s_n \cdots s_1$ for some $s_i\in S$. The *palindromic
length* is the word norm associated with the generating set consisting of
all palindromes. A conjugate of a palindrome is not a palindrome in general
but it is a product of two palindromes. That is why the palindromic length
is a conjugation invariant norm.

---------

<a href="verbal-norm">
</a>
**Verbal norm.** Let $w\in \mathbf F_n$ be a reduced word in the free group
of rank $n$. Let $S = \{s \in G\ | \ s=\psi(w^{\pm 1})\}$, where 
$\psi\colon \mathbf F_n\to G$ is a homomorphism. The word norm on the group
$G_S$ generated by $S$ is called the *verbal norm*. It is invariant with respect
to all automorphisms of $G$ preserving $G_S$. In particular, if $G_S=G$ then the
verbal norm is $\mathsf{Aut}(G)$-invariant.

--------

<a href="volume-support">
</a>
**Volume of the support.** Let $f\in {\rm Diff}(M,{\rm vol})$ be a volume
preserving diffeomorphism the volume of the support of $f$ defines a norm 
$\operatorname{vol-supp}$ on the group ${\rm Diff}(M,{\rm vol})$.  

**_Facts:_**

* The $\operatorname{vol-supp}$ norm is non-discrete; indeed, there are diffeomorphisms
  of supported on sets of arbitrarily small positive volume.  
* The cyclic subgroup $\langle f\rangle$ is bounded by $\operatorname{vol-supp}(f)$.
* If $M$ is of infinite volume then the diameter of $\operatorname{vol-supp}$ is
  infinite. 



---------

<a href="word-norm">
</a>
**The word norm.** If $G$ is generated by finitely many conjugacy classes then
the Lipschitz class of the associated word norm is maximal among all conjugation
invariant norms on $G$ (see [Basic Facts](basic-facts.html)). It is called
**the** *word norm* on $G$.
