Embeddings

The cancellation Cayley graph contains large and varied metric configurations. Each result specifies the metric and the scope of the embedding.

  • The countable Hamming cube

    Let \(\mathcal H\) be the finite subsets of \(\mathbb N\) with distance \(d(A,B)=|A\triangle B|\); equivalently it is the finite-support binary cube with its \(\ell^1\) metric.

    Theorem III.16 (BGKM). There is an isometric embedding \(\mathcal H\to(F_2,d_{\mathrm{c}})\). [BGKM], Theorem 3.F. The locally finite tree consequence is Theorem 1.D.

    Proof

    Construction outline. Extend an embedded finite cube by placing a translated copy beside it. Use a new conjugate \(b^{4k}ab^{-4k}\) with \(k\) larger than the ordinary lengths already involved. In a minimal cancellation scheme crossing between the two copies, the new central \(a\)-letter must be deleted: pairing it to an old letter would require too many deletions across the long \(b\)-block. Thus cross-distances increase by exactly one. Iterating with compatible extensions embeds the union of finite cubes. The blocking estimate is proved in [BGKM], Theorem 3.F.

  • CAT(0) cube complexes
    Corollary III.17. The vertex set of every CAT(0) cube complex with countably many vertices embeds isometrically into \((F_2,d_{\mathrm{c}})\) , with its combinatorial edge metric. AI-written deduction from III.16 and the classical hyperplane distance formula; see [NR], §3.
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Choose a vertex \(o\). For each vertex \(v\), let \(A(v)\) be the set of hyperplanes separating \(o\) from \(v\). This is finite. The hyperplane distance formula says that graph distance is the number of separating hyperplanes, so \[ d(v,w)=|A(v)\triangle A(w)|. \tag{III.15} \] There are at most countably many hyperplanes, since each crosses an edge and the graph has countably many edges. Hence \(v\mapsto A(v)\) maps isometrically into \(\mathcal H\); compose with III.16. This is the combinatorial \(\ell^1\) metric, not the piecewise Euclidean CAT(0) metric. No assertion about edge-interior distances is needed.

  • The countable-rank free abelian group
    Corollary III.18. The metric space \((\bigoplus_{i\ge1}\mathbb Z,\|\cdot\|_1)\) embeds isometrically into \((F_2,d_{\mathrm{c}})\) . The embedding is not asserted to be a homomorphism.
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Use coordinates \((i,+,j)\) and \((i,-,j)\), with \(i,j\ge1\). Encode an integer \(r\ge0\) in coordinate \(i\) by the first \(r\) positive slots, and \(r<0\) by the first \(-r\) negative slots. Symmetric difference has size \(|r-s|\): for equal signs the sets are nested, and for opposite signs they are disjoint. A finite-support integer vector produces a finite subset of this countable slot set. Summing over coordinates proves an isometric map to \(\mathcal H\), and III.16 finishes the proof. An injective homomorphism is impossible already for \(\mathbb Z^2\), since abelian subgroups of a free group are cyclic.

  • Word graphs and word complexes

    To make “word complex” precise in this first version, let \(W(S)\) be the set of finite positive words, with an edge for one insertion or deletion. Its vertex metric is \[ d_{\mathrm{edit}}(u,v)=|u|+|v|-2\operatorname{LCS}(u,v), \tag{III.16} \] where LCS denotes the length of a longest common subsequence. Injective words form the subgraph in which each symbol occurs at most once. The empty word is included. This graph is not the ordinary 1-skeleton of the usual cell complex whose cells are injective words: it is its augmented face-incidence graph restricted to single-letter changes.

    Proposition III.19. The positive-word graph embeds isometrically into \((F(S),d_{\mathrm{c}})\) by evaluation. The same holds for injective words and for bounded-multiplicity word graphs. For every fixed \(r\) , words of length at most \(r\) over a countable alphabet embed isometrically into \((F_2,d_{\mathrm{c}})\) . AI-written argument; the rank-two transfer uses [KLN], Lemma 4.1.
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    An insertion or deletion changes the represented group element by a conjugate of a basis letter, giving the upper bound. Conversely, consider a noncrossing inverse pairing for \(u^{-1}v\). No two letters within \(u^{-1}\) can pair, and no two within \(v\) can pair, because each block has only one sign. All pairs connect the blocks. Noncrossing forces their matched symbols to occur in the same order in \(u\) and \(v\); thus they form a common subsequence. There are at most \(\operatorname{LCS}(u,v)\) pairs, leaving at least \(|u|+|v|-2\operatorname{LCS}(u,v)\) unpaired positions. This is the lower bound in (III.16). Deleting to an LCS and then inserting remains in the injective or bounded-multiplicity class, so the intrinsic graph metrics agree.

    For the rank-two statement, [KLN], Lemma 4.1, supplies, for \(m\ge2\), a homomorphism \(\Psi_m\colon F(S)\to F(a,b)\) preserving the norm of every element represented by a word of ordinary length at most \(m\). Explicitly enumerate \(S=\{s_i\}\) and use \(\Psi_m(s_i)=b^{-mi}ab^{mi}\). Take \(m\ge2r\). Every difference \(u^{-1}v\) under consideration has length at most \(2r\), so all pairwise distances are preserved.

    Scope: this proves a separate rank-two embedding for each length bound. These maps need not be compatible as the bound grows. A single isometric embedding of the entire unbounded word graph into \(F_2\) is not established by this argument. Likewise, an arbitrary chosen subcomplex can have longer intrinsic paths than the ambient word graph; induced metric and intrinsic graph metric must be distinguished.
  • Symmetric groups and the Ulam metric

    Represent \(\sigma\in S_n\) as the positive word \(\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(n)\). Use the conventional Ulam metric \[ d_U(\sigma,\pi)=n-\operatorname{LCS}(\sigma,\pi). \tag{III.17} \] This is the minimum number of moves which remove one symbol and reinsert it elsewhere. It is not the number of separate insertions and deletions.

    Corollary III.20 (the scale factor). For every \(n\) , the word construction followed by III.19 embeds \((S_n,2d_U)\) isometrically into \((F_2,d_{\mathrm{c}})\) . Equivalently it is an isometric embedding of \((S_n,d_U)\) into \((F_2,\tfrac12d_{\mathrm{c}})\) .
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Comparing (III.16) with (III.17), both words have length \(n\), so III.19 gives cancellation distance \(2n-2\operatorname{LCS}(\sigma,\pi)=2d_U(\sigma,\pi)\). Apply its rank-two transfer with \(r=n\).

  • Obstruction for the unscaled Ulam metric
    Proposition III.21 (unscaled obstruction). For \(n\ge3\) , there is no isometric embedding of \((S_n,d_U)\) into the vertex set of the basis cancellation Cayley graph of any free group.
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Send every basis generator to \(1\in\mathbb Z/2\). Every normal generator also maps to \(1\), so each Cayley edge changes parity. The graph is bipartite and has no triangle. But the three permutations \(123\), \(213\), \(132\) have pairwise Ulam distance one. Append \(4,\ldots,n\) for larger \(n\). An isometric vertex embedding would make them three pairwise adjacent vertices, a contradiction.

  • Related Hilbert-space question

    The draft [H] proposes a single Hilbert-valued map on positive words such that, if every symbol appears at most \(M\) times in each word and \(D=d_{\mathrm{edit}}(u,v)\), then \[ c\log(1+D/M)\le\|F(u)-F(v)\|^2\le C\log(1+D). \tag{III.18} \]

    AI-generated result from [H]. Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Proof outline from [H]. At each dyadic probability \(p=2^{-j}\) retain each alphabet symbol independently and keep all its occurrences. Use an orthonormal vector indexed by the restricted word, centered at the empty word, in an \(L^2\) space, and sum orthogonally over \(j\). Squared distance is twice the sum of disagreement probabilities. An LCS deletion set gives an upper bound \(\min\{1,pD\}\). For a lower bound, use differing letter counts and a maximal matching in the graph of two-letter projection disagreements. Bounded multiplicity gives at least \(D/(4M)\) independent one- or two-symbol witnesses, hence disagreement probability at least \(1-e^{-p^2D/(4M)}\). Summing dyadic scales gives the stated logarithmic bounds. The full estimates, constants, and limitations are in [H].

    For fixed \(M\) these controls give a coarse embedding, including injective words and permutation spaces. This does not decide coarse embeddability of the full cancellation-metric free group. The multiplicity bound, the fixed-length rank-two maps, and the distinction between a subspace and the entire group all matter.
