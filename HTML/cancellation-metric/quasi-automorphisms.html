Quasi-automorphisms

Explore inverse-preserving representatives, the outer quotient, and its subgroups. The research statements on this page retain their verification records.

  • Definitions

    Let \(\operatorname{qAut}(F_n)\) be the group of inverse-preserving bijections whose maps and inverses are quasi-homomorphisms. Put \[ N_n=\{f\in\operatorname{qAut}(F_n):\sup_gd_{\mathrm{c}}(f(g),g)<\infty\},\qquad \operatorname{qOut}(F_n)=\operatorname{qAut}(F_n)/N_n. \] Composition respects bounded equivalence by II.3; in particular \(N_n\) is normal. Explicitly, if \(f\) moves points by at most \(A\), then \(hfh^{-1}\) moves them by at most \(\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{c}}(h)A\).

  • Representatives, cardinality and torsion
    Theorem III.7 (bijective representatives). Every quasi-isomorphism between countable nonabelian free groups with bi-invariant metrics is equivalent to an inverse-preserving bijection fixing \(1\) , with its inverse equivalent to the specified quasi-inverse. [Q], theorem “Bijective and inverse-preserving representatives”, specialized to free groups.
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Write \(q\colon H\to G\) and \(r\colon G\to H\) with \(d(rq(x),x)\le C_H\) and \(d(qr(y),y)\le C_G\). Choose infinite conjugacy classes \(A\subset H\), \(B\subset G\). Their norms are bounded, say by \(u,v\); the images under \(q,r\) are bounded, say by \(M_q,M_r\), by the conjugacy estimate in the proof of II.3 (which does not need a finite basis).

    Declare \((x,y)\) admissible if \(d(q(x),y)\le R\) and \(d(x,r(y))\le S\), where \[ R=\max\{v,C_G+D_q+M_q\},\qquad S=\max\{u,C_H+D_r+M_r\}. \] For fixed \(x\), the infinitely many \(y=q(x)c\), \(c\in B\), are admissible. For fixed \(y\), the infinitely many \(x=r(y)a\), \(a\in A\), are admissible. Enumerate both groups. Starting with \((1,1)\), alternately match the first unused element on either side to an unused admissible partner, and match their inverses at the same step. Free groups have no nonidentity involutions, so these assignments do not conflict. Only finitely many choices are excluded at each step.

    Inverse pairs obey enlarged uniform bounds because \(d(q(x^{-1}),q(x)^{-1})\le D_q+\|q(1)\|\), and likewise for \(r\). The resulting perfect matching defines \(b\) with \(b\sim q\) and \(b^{-1}\sim r\). The bounded-perturbation estimate makes both maps quasi-homomorphisms.

  • Cardinality and involutions
    Theorem III.8. There is an injection \(\prod_{k\ge1}C_2\hookrightarrow\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\) . Consequently \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\) has cardinality continuum and contains continuum many involutions. [Q], syllable substitutions and cardinality.
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Write \(F_n=\langle a\rangle*K\), choosing \(b\in K\) from the basis. For \(E\subseteq\mathbb N_{\ge1}\), let \(p_E\) interchange \(2k\) and \(2k+1\) when \(k\in E\), extend oddly to negative integers, and fix all other integers. Replace every maximal \(a\)-syllable \(a^r\) by \(a^{p_E(r)}\), leaving \(K\)-syllables unchanged; call the map \(B_E\).

    The substitutions preserve inverse pairs and are involutions. After maximal syllable cancellation in a product, at most one merger remains. Its exponent discrepancy is at most three because \(|p_E(r)-r|\le1\). Hence \(D_{B_E}\le3\). Moreover \(B_EB_F=B_{E\triangle F}\).

    If \(k\in E\triangle F\), evaluate at \(w_N=(a^{2k}b)^N\). The \(a\)-exponent sums of its two images differ by \(N\). Since exponent sum is \(1\)-Lipschitz, their distance is at least \(N\), so the classes differ. This proves injectivity. The upper cardinality bound follows because there are only continuum many maps from a countable set to itself.

  • Subgroups and relations

    Let \(R_n\) consist of classes with a rational representative: the graph on reduced input and reduced output words is recognized by a finite transducer. Let \(Q_n^{\mathrm{w}}\le\operatorname{qAut}(F_n)\) consist of maps which are also quasi-isometries for the ordinary word metric, and let \(\overline Q_n^{\mathrm{w}}\) be its image in \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\).

    Theorem III.9 (subgroup picture). In the terminology above:

    • \(R_n\) is countably infinite and not normal in \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\).
    • \(\operatorname{Out}(F_n)\) injects into \(R_n\).
    • Assuming III.4, the groups \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_m)\) and \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\) are isomorphic for finite \(m,n\ge2\); hence every \(\operatorname{Out}(F_m)\) embeds in \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_2)\).
    • \(Q_n^{\mathrm{w}}\) and \(\overline Q_n^{\mathrm{w}}\) have cardinality continuum; the latter contains the product in III.8.
    • \(R_n\cap\overline Q_n^{\mathrm{w}}\) is countably infinite and contains \(\operatorname{Out}(F_n)\) and \(\bigoplus_{k\ge1}C_2\).
    • \([\operatorname{qAut}(F_n):Q_n^{\mathrm{w}}]=2^{\aleph_0}\).
    AI-generated results from [Q], finite-state representatives, outer automorphisms, and simultaneous quasi-isometries.
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Rational representatives. Finite transducers have finite descriptions, so there are only countably many. Rational relations are closed under composition and converse; restricting to reduced words preserves rationality. Thus the eligible classes form a subgroup. Finite-support exponent permutations from III.8 can be implemented by a finite buffer on \(a\)-runs, giving an embedded direct sum and infinitude.

    Non-normality. Let \(\sigma\) swap \(a,b\). The conjugates \(C_E=B_E\sigma B_E\) satisfy \[ C_E((a^{2k}b)^N)=(b^{p_E(2k)}a)^N. \] Distinct \(E,F\) therefore give inequivalent conjugates, detected by \(b\)-exponent sums. A countable subgroup containing \([\sigma]\) cannot contain this continuum-sized conjugacy class.

    Outer automorphisms. Automorphisms and inverses are Lipschitz for both metrics. Inner automorphisms have displacement at most \(2\|u\|\). Conversely, if \(\alpha\) has bounded cancellation displacement, apply any homogeneous real quasimorphism \(q\) to \(\alpha(g^k)\) and \(g^k\). Lipschitz continuity gives \(k|q(\alpha(g))-q(g)|=O(1)\), so \(q(\alpha(g))=q(g)\). The separation of conjugacy classes by homogeneous quasimorphisms in free groups (using [KL1], Lemma 3.3) makes \(\alpha\) class-preserving. Grossman’s Property A [G] makes it inner. Automorphisms have rational reduced-word graphs: the bounded-cancellation lemma permits a finite terminal buffer when reading basis letters; see [C], p. 454, and the detailed argument in [Q]. This last step uses the classical bounded-cancellation lemma as an external input.

    Ordinary metric. The maps \(B_E\) are \(3\)-bi-Lipschitz for \(d_{\mathrm{w}}\): appending a basis letter changes a terminal exponent by at most three, or adds or deletes one fixed non-\(a\) letter. This proves continuum cardinality in both groups and the stated direct-product inclusion. Countability and the finite-support maps give the intersection assertion.

    Index before quotienting. For \(E\subseteq\mathbb N_{\ge1}\), interchange \(b^{\pm k}\) with \(ab^{\pm k}a^{-1}\) for \(k\in E\), fixing everything else; call this involution \(T_E\). It moves points by at most two in cancellation distance, so lies in \(N_n\). If \(E\) is finite it is a word-metric quasi-isometry. If \(E\) is infinite, the adjacent pair \(b^k,b^ka\) has image distance \(2k+3\) for arbitrarily large \(k\in E\), so it is not. As \(T_ET_F=T_{E\triangle F}\), cosets agree exactly when \(E\triangle F\) is finite. There are continuum many such equivalence classes.

    The index assertion is before taking the quotient by bounded cancellation distance. It does not prove that \(\overline Q_n^{\mathrm{w}}\) is proper in \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\). Nor does a rational representative that fails to be a word-metric quasi-isometry show that its class has no better representative. Whether \(R_n\subseteq\overline Q_n^{\mathrm{w}}\) is left unresolved in [Q]. Simplicity of \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\) is not settled here.
