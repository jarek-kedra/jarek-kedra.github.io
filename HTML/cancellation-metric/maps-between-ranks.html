Maps between ranks

Homomorphisms are rigid, but maps with bounded multiplicative defect can behave differently. This page separates the published rigidity theorem from the proposed flexibility construction.

  • Algebraic rigidity

    Theorem III.3 (Kędra–Libman). A homomorphism \(F_m\to F_n\) between finite-rank nonabelian free groups, with their basis cancellation metrics, is a quasi-isometry if and only if it is an isomorphism. Published: [KL1], Theorem 1.1.

    Proof

    An isomorphism and its inverse are Lipschitz by the homomorphism estimate. A noninjective homomorphism kills a nontrivial cyclic subgroup, unbounded by III.1, so cannot be a quasi-isometric embedding. The remaining, nontrivial part of the published proof shows that a proper image of a homomorphism from a finite-rank free group is not coarsely dense in the nonabelian target; see [KL1], Propositions 3.4 and 3.6. This excludes injective nonsurjective homomorphisms as well.

  • Quasi-algebraic flexibility
    Theorem III.4 (proposed finite-rank flexibility). For every finite \(m,n\ge2\) there is an inverse-preserving bijection \(f\colon F_m\to F_n\) , fixing \(1\) , such that \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\) are Lipschitz quasi-homomorphisms for cancellation metrics.     Proof

    The next subsection constructs exact inverse maps \(\Psi\colon F_3\to F_2\) and \(\Phi\colon F_2\to F_3\). The junction estimates below give defects at most \(3\) and \(30\). Their values on basis letters have norms at most \(2\) and \(1\), respectively, so II.3 gives Lipschitz constants \(11\) and \(91\).

    Extend either map to \(F_3*F_r\leftrightarrow F_2*F_r\) by applying it to syllables in the first factor and fixing the other factor. This remains an inverse-preserving bijection. In a product of two free-product normal forms, first remove the maximal inverse syllable pairs. Their images cancel exactly. There is then at most one nontrivial syllable merger, whose defect is bounded by the defect of the original map; a merger in the fixed factor has zero error. Thus the extended map and its inverse have finite defect. Their basis images are bounded, so they are Lipschitz. Finite composition changes rank one step at a time and proves the theorem. This argument makes no claim of constants independent of the ranks.

  • An explicit construction

    Read each maximal \(x\)-run as one atom, and each individual \(z\)-letter as one atom. A reduced word with atoms has unique form \[ w=y^{r_0}U_1y^{r_1}\cdots U_ky^{r_k},\qquad U_i\in\{x^p:p\ne0\}\cup\{z,z^{-1}\}. \] A gap between two \(x\)-atoms, or inverse \(z\)-atoms, must be nonzero. Every other internal gap may be zero and has sign \(\varepsilon\), the sign of its \(z\)-atom or common sign of its two \(z\)-atoms.

    Set \[ \theta(x^p)=a^{p+\operatorname{sgn}(p)},\qquad\theta(z^\varepsilon)=a^\varepsilon, \tag{III.4} \] \[ \mu_\varepsilon(r)=\begin{cases}r+\varepsilon&\varepsilon r\ge0,\\r&\varepsilon r<0.\end{cases} \tag{III.5} \] For zero-forbidden gaps let \(\gamma_{U,V}\) be the identity; for signed zero-allowed gaps let it be \(\mu_\varepsilon\). Leave both endpoint gaps unchanged. Define \[ \Psi(w)=b^{r_0}\theta(U_1)b^{\gamma_{U_1,U_2}(r_1)}\cdots\theta(U_k)b^{r_k},\qquad\Psi(y^r)=b^r. \tag{III.6} \] The internal output gaps are nonzero. Decode a nonzero \(a\)-run using \[ \beta(p)=\begin{cases}z^p&|p|=1,\\x^{p-\operatorname{sgn}(p)}&|p|\ge2,\end{cases} \tag{III.7} \] and a signed internal \(b\)-gap using \[ \nu_\varepsilon(q)=\begin{cases}q-\varepsilon&\varepsilon q>0,\\q&\varepsilon q<0.\end{cases} \tag{III.8} \] The other gaps remain unchanged. This defines \(\Phi\).

    Input Output under \(\Psi\) Reason
    \(x\) \(a^2\) one \(x\)-atom
    \(z^2\) \(aba\) two \(z\)-atoms and a corrected zero gap
    \(xz\) \(a^2ba\) positive signed zero gap
    \(xy^{-1}z\) \(a^2b^{-1}a\) negative gap unchanged
    \(xyx\) \(a^2ba^2\) gap between two \(x\)-atoms unchanged
    Proposition III.5 (exact inverse and defect estimates). The displayed maps fix \(1\) , preserve inverses, are mutually inverse, and satisfy \[ D_\Psi\le3,\quad D_\Phi\le30,\quad\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Psi)\le11,\quad\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Phi)\le91. \tag{III.9} \] AI-generated argument from [R], §§4–6.

    Proof

    The maps \(\theta\) and \(\beta\) are inverse atom lookups by (III.4) and (III.7); \(\mu_\varepsilon\colon \mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\) and \(\nu_\varepsilon\) are inverse bijections by (III.5) and (III.8). Decoding therefore recovers exactly the admissible atomic data. Since \(\mu_{-\varepsilon}(-r)=-\mu_\varepsilon(r)\) and the same identity holds for \(\nu\), reversing and inverting the data commutes with both maps.

    For a reduced concatenation \(uv\), the forward map changes only the junction. If two \(x\)-runs merge, their signs agree and their encoded exponent sums differ by one. Otherwise, only the new internal gap can require a correction, also of size at most one. Neighboring signed gap types remain unchanged in the merger case because the merged atom is still an \(x\)-atom. Hence the forward junction cost is at most one.

    For the inverse map, a reduced concatenation either creates one internal gap (cost at most one) or merges two same-sign \(a\)-runs. In the latter case replace \(\beta(p)\beta(q)\) by \(\beta(p+q)\). Each \(\beta(t)\) lies within cancellation distance two of \(x^t\), so the product estimate (II.2) bounds this replacement by six. Only the gaps directly before and after the merged atom can change type. Each decoding rule differs from the original exponent by at most one, so changing the rule costs at most two on each side. The total junction cost is at most \(2+6+2=10\).

    Finally write arbitrary reduced inputs as \(g=pc\), \(h=c^{-1}r\), with \(pr\) reduced, choosing maximal cancellation. Three reduced-junction comparisons, on \(pc,c^{-1}r,pr\), cost at most three times the local bound; the middle images of \(c,c^{-1}\) cancel exactly. Thus the global defects are at most \(3\) and \(30\). Apply II.3 with the stated basis-image bounds for the Lipschitz estimates in (III.9).

  • Transducers and the ordinary word metric
    Proposition III.6. On freely reduced inputs \(\Psi\) is realized by a thirteen-state sequential transducer. Its inverse is a rational transduction. Both \(\Psi\) and \(\Phi\) are \(3\) -Lipschitz for \(d_{\mathrm{w}}\) . [T], explicit sequential realization; [Q], proposition labelled “recoding-word”.
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Use an initial state \(I\) and states \((T,s)\), where \(T\in\{X_+,X_-,Z_+,Z_-\}\) records the last atom type and sign and \(s\in\{0,+,-\}\) records the sign of the following \(y\)-gap. Copy every \(y^{\pm1}\) immediately to \(b^{\pm1}\). The first letter of an \(x\)-run emits \(a^{\pm2}\); each continuation emits \(a^{\pm1}\); a \(z\)-letter emits \(a^{\pm1}\). Just before a new atom, emit an extra \(b^\varepsilon\) exactly when its gap is signed \(\varepsilon\) and \(s\) is \(0\) or \(\varepsilon\). No terminal output is needed. These rules give the defining atom-and-gap formula. Restriction to the regular language of reduced words gives a rational graph. Swapping input and output labels gives a rational relation which is the graph of the inverse because the function is bijective. This does not assert that the inverse is sequential without delay.

    For the ordinary metric check right multiplication by a basis letter. In \(\Psi\) a surviving terminal \(x\)-run changes encoded length by one. Creating or deleting a terminal atom costs at most two letters, and converting its adjacent gap between endpoint and internal status costs at most one. A terminal \(y\)-change costs one. In \(\Phi\) most terminal changes cost at most two. The remaining transition changes an \(a\)-run between exponent \(\varepsilon\) and \(2\varepsilon\), so the decoded atom changes between \(z^\varepsilon\) and \(x^\varepsilon\) (cost two), and its preceding gap rule changes by at most one. Hence every edge has output distance at most three in either direction. Sum along word-metric geodesics.
