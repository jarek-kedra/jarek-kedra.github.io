Stable length

Stable cancellation length measures the growth of powers of a fixed element. The main results here concern its positivity and rationality.

  • Stable cancellation length

    Define \[ \tau(g)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\|g^k\|}{k}=\inf_{k\ge1}\frac{\|g^k\|}{k}. \tag{III.1} \] The limit exists by subadditivity. It is conjugation invariant, satisfies \(\tau(g^{-1})=\tau(g)\) and \(\tau(g^m)=|m|\tau(g)\), and is at most \(\|g\|\). These properties alone do not justify calling it a norm: a triangle inequality for noncommuting elements does not follow by taking powers.

  • Non-triviality and spectral gap
    Theorem III.1 (gap at zero). For every nonidentity \(g\in F(S)\) , \[ \tau(g)\ge1. \tag{III.2} \] The constant is sharp, since \(\tau(a)=1\) for a basis generator. Thus every nontrivial cyclic subgroup is undistorted for the cancellation metric. AI-written deduction below from the classical Duncan–Howie commutator-length bound [DH]. This deduction is not attributed to [DH] as a statement about cancellation length.
    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Let \(\operatorname{ab}\colon F(S)\to\mathbb Z^{(S)}\) be abelianization with the \(\ell^1\) norm. It is \(1\)-Lipschitz for cancellation length. If \(\operatorname{ab}(g)\ne0\), then \(\|g^k\|\ge k\|\operatorname{ab}(g)\|_1\ge k\).

    Suppose \(g\in[F,F]\setminus\{1\}\). We first prove \(\operatorname{cl}(h)\le\|h\|/2\) for every \(h\in[F,F]\), where \(\operatorname{cl}\) denotes the minimum number of commutators in a product representing \(h\). Take a shortest factorization of \(h\) into \(N\) conjugates of signed basis generators. Abelianization shows that, for each generator, positive and negative occurrences have equal counts. Pair them. Realize the factorization by a map of a planar surface with \(N+1\) boundary components to the basis rose: one boundary reads \(h\) and the others read the signed generators, with paths to the basepoint recording the conjugators. Glue each oppositely oriented pair by an annulus mapping to its generator circle. The resulting orientable surface has one boundary, Euler characteristic \(1-N\), and genus \(N/2\). Hence \(\operatorname{cl}(h)\le N/2\).

    Duncan–Howie’s theorem gives \(k\le2\operatorname{cl}(g^k)-1\) for a nontrivial \(k\)-th power in a free group. Therefore \(\|g^k\|\ge2\operatorname{cl}(g^k)\ge k+1\), and division by \(k\) gives the claim. The inequality also gives \(d_{\mathrm{c}}(g^p,g^q)\ge|p-q|\), while subadditivity gives the upper bound \(|p-q|\|g\|\).

  • Jaspars’s rationality theorem

    Theorem III.2 (Jaspars). Stable cancellation length takes rational values on free groups. More generally the power-product length sequences in Theorem 1.1 of [J] are uniformly semi-arithmetic. Published: [J], Theorem 1.1.

    Proof

    Proof outline, not a replacement for the paper. Encode words and deletion certificates by context-free languages. Parikh’s theorem converts the relevant counting information into semilinear sets. The resulting integer optimization has an eventual periodic arithmetic structure; its asymptotic slope is rational. The substantial language construction and semi-arithmetic argument are in [J], §§2–4.

    For orientation, [J], Examples 1.3(2), gives \[ \|[a,b]^k\|=2\lfloor k/2\rfloor+2\quad(k\ge1),\qquad \tau([a,b])=1. \tag{III.3} \] Rationality is a statement about each fixed element. It does not give a global coarse embedding or a classification of the entire metric space.
