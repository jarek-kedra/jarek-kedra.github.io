  • Metric identities and commutator bound

    Proposition II.1. Let \(G\) be a group with a bi-invariant metric \(d\), and put \(\|g\|=d(1,g)\). For all \(u,v,u',v',L,R\in G\),

    \[d(uv,u'v')\le d(u,u')+d(v,v'),\qquad d(u^{-1},v^{-1})=d(u,v).\tag{II.2}\]

    \[d(LuR,LvR)=d(u,v),\qquad \|[u,v]\|\le2\min\{\|u\|,\|v\|\}.\tag{II.3}\]

    Here \([u,v]=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}\).

    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    For (II.2), insert \(u'v\) between \(uv\) and \(u'v'\) and apply the triangle inequality and translation invariance. Inversion follows from symmetry and conjugation invariance of the norm. Write \([u,v]=(uvu^{-1})v^{-1}\) to obtain \(2\|v\|\); the other bound follows by the alternative grouping.

  • Bounded perturbations of quasi-homomorphisms

    Let \(G\) be a group and \(H\) a group with a bi-invariant metric. Let \(q\colon G\to H\) be a quasi-homomorphism of defect \(D_q\). If \(p\colon G\to H\) satisfies \(d_H(p(x),q(x))\le A\) for all \(x\in G\), then \(p\) is a quasi-homomorphism and

    \[D_p\le D_q+3A.\]

    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    For \(x,y\in G\), insert \(q(xy)\) and \(q(x)q(y)\) between \(p(xy)\) and \(p(x)p(y)\). The triangle inequality and (II.2) give

    \[d_H(p(xy),p(x)p(y))\le A+D_q+2A.\]

    Take the supremum over \(x,y\).

  • Cancellation length and normal word length

    Theorem II.2. Let \(S\) be a set, and let \(w\) be a word in \(S^{\pm1}\). Equip \(F(S)\) with the normal word norm associated with \(S\). Then

    \[\ell_{\mathrm c}(w)=\|[w]\|.\tag{II.4}\]

    Consequently, cancellation length depends only on the represented element and defines a conjugation-invariant norm on \(F(S)\).

    Published: [BGKM], Proposition 2.A and corrected Proposition 2.E′ (arXiv v3, §6). The correction applies to the general statement of Proposition 2.E; the free-group assertion used here is covered by the corrected statement.

    Proof

    Deleting one letter replaces a product \(usu'\) by \(uu'\); their quotient is conjugate to \(s\). Thus a deletion scheme of size \(k\) produces a factorization by \(k\) normal letters, so \(\|[w]\|\le\ell_{\mathrm{c}}(w)\).

    For the converse first note that inserting or removing an adjacent inverse pair does not change the minimum deletion count. One inequality follows by keeping and cancelling the inserted pair. For the reverse inequality, use a noncrossing pairing of the undeleted letters witnessing free triviality. If both inserted positions survive, remove their pairing edges and reconnect their former partners when necessary; the inverse labels match and noncrossing is preserved. If one inserted position is deleted, delete instead the old position paired to the other one. If both are deleted, remove both deletions. This gives a scheme on the shorter word of no greater cost.

    A shortest normal factorization \(g=\prod_{i=1}^k u_i s_i u_i^{-1}\) has a word representative trivialized by deleting the displayed \(s_i\). Any other representative is related by insertion and removal of adjacent inverse pairs. Hence \(\ell_{\mathrm{c}}(w)\le k=\|g\|\).

  • Lipschitz bound for quasi-homomorphisms

    Proposition II.3. Equip \(F(S)\) with its basis cancellation metric. Let \(H\) be a group with a bi-invariant metric, and let \(q\colon F(S)\to H\) be a quasi-homomorphism of defect \(D\). Suppose that

    \[B=\sup_{s\in S^{\pm1}}\|q(s)\|<\infty.\]

    Then \(q\) is \((B+5D)\)-Lipschitz. If \(q(g^{-1})=q(g)^{-1}\) for every \(g\in F(S)\), then \(q\) is \((B+3D)\)-Lipschitz.

    AI exposition from [Q], preliminary Lipschitz lemmas, and [R], defect-to-Lipschitz lemma.

    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    The defect bound (II.1) applied to \((1,1)\) gives \(\|q(1)\|\le D\), and applied to \((u,u^{-1})\) gives \(d(q(u^{-1}),q(u)^{-1})\le2D\). Applying it twice more to \(usu^{-1}\) shows \[ \|q(usu^{-1})\|\le B+4D. \tag{II.5} \] Consequently, (II.5) gives \(d(q(gc),q(g))\le B+5D\) for every normal letter \(c\). Factor \(g^{-1}h\) into \(d_{\mathrm{c}}(g,h)\) normal letters and sum these bounds along the resulting path. If inverse preservation is exact, the \(2D\) inverse error vanishes, giving \(B+3D\). No bound on the conjugating words is used.

  • Lipschitz bound for homomorphisms

    Equip \(F(S)\) with its basis cancellation metric. Let \(H\) be a group with a bi-invariant metric, and let \(q\colon F(S)\to H\) be a homomorphism. If \(B=\sup_{s\in S}\|q(s)\|<\infty\), then \(q\) is \(B\)-Lipschitz.

    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    For \(u\in F(S)\) and \(s\in S^{\pm1}\), conjugation invariance gives \(\|q(usu^{-1})\|=\|q(s)\|\le B\). A normal factorization of \(g^{-1}h\) of minimal length therefore gives

    \[d_H(q(g),q(h))=\|q(g^{-1}h)\|\le B\|g^{-1}h\|.\]

  • Isometric inclusion of basis-generated free factors

    Let \(A\subseteq S\). Equip \(F(A)\) and \(F(S)\) with the cancellation metrics associated with \(A\) and \(S\), respectively. The canonical inclusion \(i\colon F(A)\to F(S)\) is isometric.

    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    Let \(r\colon F(S)\to F(A)\) fix the generators in \(A\) and send the generators in \(S\setminus A\) to \(1\). The homomorphism bound makes both \(i\) and \(r\) 1-Lipschitz. Since \(r\circ i=\operatorname{id}_{F(A)}\), for \(g,h\in F(A)\),

    \[d_{F(A)}(g,h)\le d_{F(S)}(i(g),i(h))\le d_{F(A)}(g,h).\]

  • Defect of a composition

    Let \(G\) be a group, and let \(H\) and \(K\) be groups with bi-invariant metrics. Let \(q\colon G\to H\) and \(p\colon H\to K\) be quasi-homomorphisms of defects \(D_q\) and \(D_p\). If \(p\) is Lipschitz, then \(p\circ q\) is a quasi-homomorphism and

    \[D_{p\circ q}\le D_p+\operatorname{Lip}(p)D_q.\tag{II.6}\]

    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    For \(x,y\in G\), insert \(p(q(x)q(y))\) between \(p(q(xy))\) and \(p(q(x))p(q(y))\). Thus

    \[d_K(p(q(xy)),p(q(x))p(q(y)))\le\operatorname{Lip}(p)D_q+D_p.\]

    Taking the supremum gives (II.6).
