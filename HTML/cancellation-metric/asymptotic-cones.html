Asymptotic cones

Rescaling a bi-invariant metric produces a complete metric group. This page connects that construction with rank flexibility, torsion, and stable length.

  • Complete metric groups

    Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter \(\omega\) on the positive integers and a sequence of positive real numbers \(s=(s_j)\) with \(s_j\to\infty\). For a group with a bi-invariant metric, write \(\|g\|=d(1,g)\). An admissible sequence satisfies \(\sup_j\|g_j\|/s_j<\infty\). The asymptotic cone, based at the identity, is \(\operatorname{Cone}_{\omega}(G,d;s)\), the set of admissible sequences modulo zero pseudodistance, where

    \[ d_{\omega}([g_j],[h_j]) =\lim_{\omega}\frac{d(g_j,h_j)}{s_j}. \tag{III.10} \]

    Proposition III.10 (the cone as a metric group). This cone is a complete topological group with a bi-invariant metric, with multiplication and inversion given by \([g_j][h_j]=[g_jh_j]\) and \([g_j]^{-1}=[g_j^{-1}]\). The original metric group need not be complete. Calegari–Zhuang [CZ], §3.2, Proposition 3.3, supplies the group construction in the verbal-metric setting. For the general formulation and completeness reference, see [KLN], §2, “Asymptotic cones”, Lemma 2.6 and the preceding paragraph.

    Proof

    The metric identities (II.2) give \(d(gh,g'h')\le d(g,g')+d(h,h')\) and \(d(g^{-1},h^{-1})=d(g,h)\). Thus products and inverses preserve admissibility and equivalence. The group axioms and bi-invariance pass to the quotient; the same estimates prove continuity of multiplication and inversion.

    For completeness, take a Cauchy sequence in the cone and pass to a subsequence \((x^k)\) with \(d_{\omega}(x^k,x^{k+1})<2^{-k}\). Choose admissible representatives \((x^k_j)_j\). For each \(k\), the set \(A_k=\{j:d(x^k_j,x^{k+1}_j)/s_j<2^{1-k}\}\) belongs to \(\omega\). Put \(E_1=\mathbb N\) and \(E_k=\{j\ge k\}\cap\bigcap_{r<k}A_r\) for \(k\ge2\). These sets decrease and belong to the ultrafilter. Let \(k(j)=\max\{k:j\in E_k\}\) and \(y_j=x^{k(j)}_j\). The maximum exists because \(k\le j\). The sum of the successive bounds is at most two, so \((y_j)\) is admissible. On \(E_k\), its scaled distance from \(x^k_j\) is at most \(\sum_{r\ge k}2^{1-r}=2^{2-k}\). Hence \(x^k\to[y_j]\). The original Cauchy sequence has the same limit.

  • Independence of finite rank

    Lemma III.11 (bounded defects disappear). Let \(f\colon G\to H\) be a Lipschitz quasi-homomorphism between groups with bi-invariant metrics. Then \(f_{\omega}([g_j])=[f(g_j)]\) is a Lipschitz group homomorphism between their cones with the same \(\omega\) and \(s\). Maps at uniformly bounded distance induce the same cone map. If \(f\) is a bijection and both it and its inverse are Lipschitz quasi-homomorphisms, the induced map is a bi-Lipschitz group isomorphism.

    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    If the Lipschitz constant is \(L\), then \(\|f(g)\|\le L\|g\|+\|f(1)\|\). This proves admissibility of image sequences. Dividing the Lipschitz inequality by \(s_j\) and taking the ultralimit proves well-definedness and the same Lipschitz bound on the cone. If \(D\) bounds the multiplicative defect,

    \[ d_{\omega}([f(g_jh_j)],[f(g_j)f(h_j)]) \le \lim_{\omega}\frac{D}{s_j}=0. \tag{III.11} \]

    Equation (III.11) shows that the induced map preserves products. The constant sequence \((f(1))\) represents the identity. The same division proves the bounded-distance assertion. In the bijective case, the map induced by \(f^{-1}\) is an exact inverse and is Lipschitz, proving the last assertion.

  • Bi-Lipschitz isomorphism of free-group cones

    Corollary III.12 (bi-Lipschitz isomorphism of free-group cones). Assuming the quasi-algebraic flexibility theorem in quasi-algebraic flexibility, for every pair of finite ranks \(m,n\ge2\), every nonprincipal ultrafilter \(\omega\), and every scaling sequence \(s_j\to\infty\), there is a bi-Lipschitz group isomorphism

    \[ \operatorname{Cone}_{\omega}(F_m,d_{\mathrm c};s) \cong \operatorname{Cone}_{\omega}(F_n,d_{\mathrm c};s). \tag{III.12} \]

    AI-written corollary of the proposed rank-change theorem [R]. Its verification status depends on that theorem; it is not asserted here as a published result.

    Proof

    Choose the bijection supplied by the flexibility theorem. Apply the preceding lemma to it and its inverse. This gives (III.12): their induced maps are mutually inverse group homomorphisms with the original Lipschitz bounds.

    Here “isomorphism” includes the group operation, as well as the bi-Lipschitz comparison of distances. The two cones use the same ultrafilter and scaling sequence. This corollary does not establish independence of either parameter, and the available bounds may depend on the ranks. No extension to infinite rank is claimed by this argument.

  • Torsion

    Theorem III.13 (Kędra–Libman–Nie). The asymptotic cone of a nonabelian free group with its basis cancellation metric, using scaling \(n\), has nonidentity elements of order two for every nonprincipal ultrafilter. Public preprint: [KLN], Theorem 1.1.

    Proof

    Proof outline. The construction supplies elements \(f_n\in F_2\) with \(\|f_n\|=\Theta(n)\) and \(\|f_n^2\|=o(n)\). Thus their cone class is nontrivial but its square is trivial. The high-rank construction and the transfer to rank two are the substantive steps; see [KLN], Theorems 1.2 and 3.1, Lemma 4.1 and §5. There is no conflict with III.1: \(f_n\) varies with \(n\), whereas stable length fixes one element before taking powers.

  • Stable length and real characters

    Proposition III.14 (one-parameter subgroups from powers). For any fixed \(g\in G\), the map \(\iota_g(t)=[g^{\lfloor ts_j\rfloor}]\) is a homomorphism from \((\mathbb R,+)\) to the cone, and

    \[ d_{\omega}(\iota_g(t),\iota_g(u)) =|t-u|\,\tau(g), \qquad \tau(g)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\|g^k\|}{k}. \tag{III.13} \]

    Consequently, every nontrivial element of a free group with the cancellation norm determines an embedded real one-parameter subgroup, by the positive stable-length result in stable length. This is an elementary deduction; compare the power-direction construction in [CZ], §§3.3–3.4.

    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    The inequality \(\|g^k\|\le |k|\|g\|\) proves admissibility. The integers \(\lfloor ts_j\rfloor+\lfloor us_j\rfloor\) and \(\lfloor(t+u)s_j\rfloor\) differ by at most one. The product error therefore has norm at most \(\|g\|\), which disappears after scaling. If \(t\ne u\), put \(k_j=|\lfloor ts_j\rfloor-\lfloor us_j\rfloor|\). Then \(k_j\to\infty\) and \(k_j/s_j\to|t-u|\). Hence \(\|g^{k_j}\|/s_j\to\tau(g)|t-u|\). Together with (III.10), this proves (III.13). The case \(t=u\) is immediate.

  • Real characters from quasimorphisms

    Corollary III.15 (quasimorphisms give real characters). A real-valued quasimorphism \(q\) that is Lipschitz for the bi-invariant metric induces a Lipschitz homomorphism

    \[ Q_{\omega}\colon \operatorname{Cone}_{\omega}(G,d;s)\longrightarrow\mathbb R, \qquad Q_{\omega}([g_j])=\lim_{\omega}\frac{q(g_j)}{s_j}. \tag{III.14} \]

    If \(q\) is homogeneous, then \(Q_{\omega}(\iota_g(t))=tq(g)\). Every torsion element of the cone lies in the kernel of every such character. AI-written specialization and extension to general scaling of the bounded-defect argument; compare [K], Proposition 1.13(2), for the sublinear-defect formulation.

    Lean verified: no
    Human verified: no
    Proof

    In (III.14), Lipschitz continuity makes the rescaled values bounded and their ultralimit independent of the representative. The quasimorphism defect divided by \(s_j\) tends to zero, so the limit is additive. Homogeneity gives the displayed evaluation on powers. Finally, if \(x^r=1\) with \(r\ge1\), then \(rQ_{\omega}(x)=Q_{\omega}(x^r)=0\), and therefore \(Q_{\omega}(x)=0\).

    The KLN torsion elements thus cannot belong to any of the nontrivial subgroups \(\iota_g(\mathbb R)\) and cannot be detected by real characters. Fixed-element stable length and varying-sequence torsion describe different features of the same cone.

  • Directional cones

    Kędra–Libman’s directional asymptotic cone is a separate construction avoiding ultrafilters. It is a contractible group with a complete bi-invariant metric and is separable when the original group is countable. There is a canonical Lipschitz homomorphism to the corresponding ultralimit cone; see [KL2], §§6 and 9. These properties should not be transferred to the ordinary cone without a further argument. In particular, the existence of that comparison homomorphism alone gives neither injectivity nor surjectivity.
