Definitions

Notation

Let \(G\) and \(H\) be groups, with identity denoted by \(1\). Put \(\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\). Let \(S\) be a set and \(F(S)\) the free group with basis \(S\). Write \(S^{\pm1}=S\cup S^{-1}\). For a word \(w\) in \(S^{\pm1}\), denote by \([w]\) its value in \(F(S)\).

Main concepts

  • Norm

    A norm on \(G\) is a function \(\nu\colon G\to[0,\infty)\) satisfying, for all \(g,h\in G\),

    1. \(\nu(g)=0\) if and only if \(g=1\);
    2. \(\nu(g^{-1})=\nu(g)\);
    3. \(\nu(gh)\le\nu(g)+\nu(h)\).
  • Conjugation-invariant norm

    A norm \(\nu\) on \(G\) is conjugation-invariant if

    \[\nu(ugu^{-1})=\nu(g)\qquad(u,g\in G).\]

    The associated metric is \(d_\nu(g,h)=\nu(g^{-1}h)\).

  • Bi-invariant metric

    A metric \(d\) on \(G\) is bi-invariant if

    \[d(ug,uh)=d(gu,hu)=d(g,h)\qquad(u,g,h\in G).\]

    The associated norm is \(\nu_d(g)=d(1,g)\).

  • Word norm and word metric

    Let \(T\subseteq G\setminus\{1\}\) generate \(G\), and suppose that \(T=T^{-1}\). The word norm associated with \(T\) is

    \[|g|_T=\min\{k\in\mathbb N_0:g=t_1\cdots t_k,\ t_i\in T\}.\]

    The product with no factors is \(1\). The associated word metric is \(d_T(g,h)=|g^{-1}h|_T\).

  • Cayley graph

    Let \(T\subseteq G\setminus\{1\}\) be a symmetric generating set. The Cayley graph \(\operatorname{Cay}(G,T)\) is the undirected graph with vertex set \(G\) and edge set

    \[\bigl\{\{g,gt\}:g\in G,\ t\in T\bigr\}.\]

    Each edge has length one. Distances between vertices are measured by the graph metric.

  • Normal word norm

    Let \(S\) be a basis of \(F(S)\). Put

    \[\overline S=\{usu^{-1}:u\in F(S),\ s\in S^{\pm1}\}.\]

    The normal word norm associated with \(S\) is \(\|g\|=|g|_{\overline S}\). Its associated metric is \(d_{\mathrm c}(g,h)=\|g^{-1}h\|\). The ordinary word metric is the word metric associated with \(S^{\pm1}\).

  • Cancellation length

    Let \(w=s_1\cdots s_N\) be a word in \(S^{\pm1}\). For \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\), let \(w\setminus I\) denote the word obtained by deleting the letters in positions belonging to \(I\), without changing the order of the remaining letters. The cancellation length of \(w\) is

    \[\ell_{\mathrm c}(w)=\min\bigl\{|I|:I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\},\ [w\setminus I]=1\bigr\}.\]

    The identification with normal word length gives \(\ell_{\mathrm c}(w)=\|[w]\|\).

  • Bounded distance between maps

    Let \(X\) be a set and \((Y,d_Y)\) a metric space. Maps \(p,q\colon X\to Y\) are at bounded distance, written \(p\sim q\), if

    \[\sup_{x\in X}d_Y(p(x),q(x))<\infty.\]

  • Quasi-homomorphism

    Let \(H\) be equipped with a bi-invariant metric \(d_H\). A map \(q\colon G\to H\) is a quasi-homomorphism if its multiplicative defect

    \[D_q=\sup_{g,h\in G}d_H(q(gh),q(g)q(h))\tag{II.1}\]

    is finite. The term quasi-representation is used with this meaning in the source manuscripts.

  • Quasimorphism

    A quasimorphism on \(G\) is a function \(q\colon G\to\mathbb R\) such that

    \[\sup_{g,h\in G}|q(gh)-q(g)-q(h)|<\infty.\]

  • Homogeneous quasimorphism

    A quasimorphism \(q\colon G\to\mathbb R\) is homogeneous if

    \[q(g^k)=kq(g)\qquad(g\in G,\ k\in\mathbb Z).\]

  • Quasi-isomorphism

    Let \(G\) and \(H\) be equipped with bi-invariant metrics. A quasi-isomorphism is a quasi-homomorphism \(q\colon G\to H\) for which there exists a quasi-homomorphism \(r\colon H\to G\) satisfying

    \[r\circ q\sim\operatorname{id}_G,\qquad q\circ r\sim\operatorname{id}_H.\]

  • Quasi-automorphism

    Let \(G\) be equipped with a bi-invariant metric. A quasi-automorphism of \(G\) is a quasi-isomorphism \(G\to G\).

    The group denoted by \(\operatorname{qAut}(F_n)\) in this website uses bijective inverse-preserving representatives; see the definition of that group.

  • Quasi-isometry

    Let \((X,d_X)\) and \((Y,d_Y)\) be metric spaces. A map \(f\colon X\to Y\) is a quasi-isometry if there exist constants \(L\ge1\), \(C\ge0\), and \(R\ge0\) such that

    1. for all \(x,x'\in X\),

      \[L^{-1}d_X(x,x')-C\le d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\le Ld_X(x,x')+C;\]

    2. for each \(y\in Y\), there exists \(x\in X\) with \(d_Y(y,f(x))\le R\).

All definitions in alphabetical order

  • Admissible sequence

    Let \((X,d,o)\) be a pointed metric space and \(s\) a scaling sequence. A sequence \((x_j)\) in \(X\) is admissible if \(\sup_j d(o,x_j)/s_j<\infty\).

    Related results and references.

  • Asymptotic cone

    Let \((X,d,o)\) be a pointed metric space, \(\omega\) a nonprincipal ultrafilter, and \(s\) a scaling sequence. On admissible sequences put

    \[\delta_\omega((x_j),(y_j))=\lim_\omega\frac{d(x_j,y_j)}{s_j}.\]

    The asymptotic cone \(\operatorname{Cone}_\omega(X,d,o;s)\) is the quotient by the relation \(\delta_\omega=0\), equipped with the metric induced by \(\delta_\omega\). For a metric group the basepoint is \(1\) and is omitted from the notation.

    Related results and references.

  • Bi-invariant metric

    A metric \(d\) on \(G\) is bi-invariant if

    \[d(ug,uh)=d(gu,hu)=d(g,h)\qquad(u,g,h\in G).\]

    The associated norm is \(\nu_d(g)=d(1,g)\).

  • Bi-Lipschitz map

    A map \(f\colon(X,d_X)\to(Y,d_Y)\) is \(L\)-bi-Lipschitz if \(L\ge1\) and

    \[L^{-1}d_X(x,x')\le d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\le Ld_X(x,x')\qquad(x,x'\in X).\]

    A bi-Lipschitz equivalence is a surjective bi-Lipschitz map. A bi-Lipschitz group isomorphism is a group isomorphism which is bi-Lipschitz for the specified metrics.

    Related results and references.

  • Bounded distance between maps

    Let \(X\) be a set and \((Y,d_Y)\) a metric space. Maps \(p,q\colon X\to Y\) are at bounded distance, written \(p\sim q\), if

    \[\sup_{x\in X}d_Y(p(x),q(x))<\infty.\]

  • Bounded-displacement subgroup

    For the basis cancellation metric on \(F_n\), put

    \[N_n=\left\{f\in\operatorname{qAut}(F_n):\sup_{g\in F_n}d_{\mathrm c}(f(g),g)<\infty\right\}.\]

    This is the subgroup used in defining \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\).

    Related results and references.

  • Bounded-multiplicity word graph

    For an integer \(M\ge1\), let \(W_M(S)\) consist of the positive words over \(S\) in which each letter occurs at most \(M\) times. Its word graph joins two words when one is obtained from the other by a single-letter insertion or deletion. The graph is equipped with its vertex path metric.

    Related results and references.

  • Cancellation length

    Let \(w=s_1\cdots s_N\) be a word in \(S^{\pm1}\). For \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\), let \(w\setminus I\) denote the word obtained by deleting the letters in positions belonging to \(I\), without changing the order of the remaining letters. The cancellation length of \(w\) is

    \[\ell_{\mathrm c}(w)=\min\bigl\{|I|:I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\},\ [w\setminus I]=1\bigr\}.\]

    The identification with normal word length gives \(\ell_{\mathrm c}(w)=\|[w]\|\).

  • CAT(0) cube complex

    A cube complex is a complex obtained by gluing Euclidean unit cubes along isometric faces. Equip it with the induced Euclidean length metric. It is CAT(0) if it is geodesic and, for every geodesic triangle, the distance between any two points on its sides is at most the distance between the corresponding points on the Euclidean comparison triangle. For vertex embeddings in this survey, the metric used is instead the combinatorial edge metric on the 1-skeleton.

    Related results and references.

  • Cayley graph

    Let \(T\subseteq G\setminus\{1\}\) be a symmetric generating set. The Cayley graph \(\operatorname{Cay}(G,T)\) is the undirected graph with vertex set \(G\) and edge set

    \[\bigl\{\{g,gt\}:g\in G,\ t\in T\bigr\}.\]

    Each edge has length one. Distances between vertices are measured by the graph metric.

  • Coarse embedding

    A map \(f\colon(X,d_X)\to(Y,d_Y)\) is a coarse embedding if there exist nondecreasing functions \(\rho_-,\rho_+\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\), with \(\rho_-(t)\to\infty\) as \(t\to\infty\), such that

    \[\rho_-(d_X(x,x'))\le d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\le\rho_+(d_X(x,x'))\qquad(x,x'\in X).\]

    Related results and references.

  • Commutator length

    Let \(G\) be a group and \(g\in[G,G]\). The commutator length \(\operatorname{cl}_G(g)\) is the least \(k\in\mathbb N_0\) such that \(g\) is a product of \(k\) commutators \([a_i,b_i]\), with \(a_i,b_i\in G\). Here \([a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}\).

    Related results and references.

  • Conjugation-invariant norm

    A norm \(\nu\) on \(G\) is conjugation-invariant if

    \[\nu(ugu^{-1})=\nu(g)\qquad(u,g\in G).\]

    The associated metric is \(d_\nu(g,h)=\nu(g^{-1}h)\).

  • Directional asymptotic cone

    The directional asymptotic cone is the metric-group construction of Kędra–Libman, [KL2], §5. Its full definition is given there. It is distinct from the ultrafilter asymptotic cone defined above.

    Related results and references.

  • Finite transducer

    Let \(A\) and \(B\) be finite alphabets. A finite transducer is a finite directed graph with specified initial and terminal states, whose edges are labelled by pairs \((u,v)\in A^*\times B^*\). A path from an initial state to a terminal state accepts the pair obtained by concatenating its input labels and its output labels. Empty labels are allowed. The accepted pairs form the relation recognized by the transducer.

    Related results and references.

  • Free abelian group with the ℓ¹ metric

    The countable-rank free abelian group is \(\bigoplus_{i\ge1}\mathbb Z\), the group of finitely supported integer sequences. Its \(\ell^1\) norm is \(\|x\|_1=\sum_i|x_i|\), and its metric is \(d_1(x,y)=\|x-y\|_1\).

    Related results and references.

  • Hamming cube, countable

    The countable Hamming cube \(\mathcal H\) is the set of finite subsets of \(\mathbb N\), with metric \(d(A,B)=|A\triangle B|\). Equivalently, it is the set of finitely supported sequences in \(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\), with the \(\ell^1\) metric.

    Related results and references.

  • Homogeneous quasimorphism

    A quasimorphism \(q\colon G\to\mathbb R\) is homogeneous if

    \[q(g^k)=kq(g)\qquad(g\in G,\ k\in\mathbb Z).\]

  • Induced metric and intrinsic graph metric

    For a subset \(A\) of a metric space \((X,d)\), its induced metric is the restriction \(d|_{A\times A}\). For a connected subgraph \(Y\) of a graph with unit edge lengths, its intrinsic graph metric is the minimum length of a path contained in \(Y\) joining the specified vertices.

    Related results and references.

  • Injective word

    A positive word is injective if no letter occurs more than once. The empty word is injective.

    Related results and references.

  • Insertion–deletion distance

    The insertion–deletion distance \(d_{\mathrm{edit}}(u,v)\) is the least number of single-letter insertions and deletions transforming \(u\) into \(v\). Equivalently,

    \[d_{\mathrm{edit}}(u,v)=|u|+|v|-2\operatorname{LCS}(u,v).\]

    Related results and references.

  • Inverse-preserving map

    A map \(f\colon G\to H\) between groups is inverse-preserving if \(f(g^{-1})=f(g)^{-1}\) for every \(g\in G\).

    Related results and references.

  • Isometric embedding

    A map \(f\colon(X,d_X)\to(Y,d_Y)\) is an isometric embedding if \(d_Y(f(x),f(x'))=d_X(x,x')\) for all \(x,x'\in X\).

    Related results and references.

  • Lipschitz map

    A map \(f\colon(X,d_X)\to(Y,d_Y)\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz if \(L\ge0\) and \(d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\le Ld_X(x,x')\) for all \(x,x'\in X\). It is Lipschitz if such an \(L\) exists. The infimum of these constants is denoted by \(\operatorname{Lip}(f)\).

    Related results and references.

  • Longest common subsequence

    A subsequence of a word is obtained by deleting zero or more positions and retaining the order of the remaining letters. For words \(u,v\), the integer \(\operatorname{LCS}(u,v)\) is the largest length of a word that is a subsequence of both \(u\) and \(v\).

    Related results and references.

  • Noncrossing inverse pairing

    Let \(w=s_1\cdots s_N\) be a word in \(S^{\pm1}\). A noncrossing inverse pairing is a set of disjoint pairs \(\{i,j\}\) of positions such that \(s_i=s_j^{-1}\), and no two pairs have endpoints \(i<k<j<\ell\). Positions outside the pairs are unpaired. A pairing of all positions witnesses free triviality; in a deletion scheme it is applied to the undeleted positions.

    Related results and references.

  • Nonprincipal ultrafilter

    An ultrafilter on \(\mathbb N\) is a family \(\omega\) of subsets of \(\mathbb N\) such that \(\varnothing\notin\omega\), \(\mathbb N\in\omega\), the family is closed under finite intersections and passage to supersets, and for every \(A\subseteq\mathbb N\) exactly one of \(A\) and \(\mathbb N\setminus A\) belongs to \(\omega\). It is nonprincipal if it contains no finite set.

    Related results and references.

  • Notation

    Let \(G\) and \(H\) be groups, with identity denoted by \(1\). Put \(\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\). Let \(S\) be a set and \(F(S)\) the free group with basis \(S\). Write \(S^{\pm1}=S\cup S^{-1}\). For a word \(w\) in \(S^{\pm1}\), denote by \([w]\) its value in \(F(S)\).

  • One-parameter subgroup

    A one-parameter subgroup of a topological group \(G\) is a continuous homomorphism \(\iota\colon(\mathbb R,+)\to G\). The power direction associated with \(g\) in the cone used in this survey is \(\iota_g(t)=[g^{\lfloor ts_j\rfloor}]\).

    Related results and references.

  • Outer automorphism group

    For a group \(G\), let \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) denote its automorphism group and \(\operatorname{Inn}(G)\) the subgroup consisting of maps \(x\mapsto gxg^{-1}\), for \(g\in G\). The outer automorphism group is \(\operatorname{Out}(G)=\operatorname{Aut}(G)/\operatorname{Inn}(G)\).

    Related results and references.

  • Outer quasi-automorphism group

    The outer quasi-automorphism group in this survey is

    \[\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)=\operatorname{qAut}(F_n)/N_n,\]

    where \(N_n\) is the bounded-displacement subgroup. Its elements are bounded-distance classes of the specified bijective representatives.

    Related results and references.

  • Positive word

    A positive word over a set \(S\) is a finite sequence of elements of \(S\), including the empty sequence. The set of positive words is denoted by \(W(S)=S^*\). The length \(|w|\) is the number of letters of \(w\).

    Related results and references.

  • Quasi-automorphism

    Let \(G\) be equipped with a bi-invariant metric. A quasi-automorphism of \(G\) is a quasi-isomorphism \(G\to G\).

    The group denoted by \(\operatorname{qAut}(F_n)\) in this website uses bijective inverse-preserving representatives; see the definition of that group.

  • Quasi-automorphism group

    Equip \(F_n\) with its basis cancellation metric. The group \(\operatorname{qAut}(F_n)\) consists of inverse-preserving bijections \(f\colon F_n\to F_n\) such that both \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\) are quasi-homomorphisms. The group operation is composition.

    Related results and references.

  • Quasi-homomorphism

    Let \(H\) be equipped with a bi-invariant metric \(d_H\). A map \(q\colon G\to H\) is a quasi-homomorphism if its multiplicative defect

    \[D_q=\sup_{g,h\in G}d_H(q(gh),q(g)q(h))\]

    is finite. The term quasi-representation is used with this meaning in the source manuscripts.

  • Quasi-isometric embedding

    A map \(f\colon(X,d_X)\to(Y,d_Y)\) is a quasi-isometric embedding if there exist \(L\ge1\) and \(C\ge0\) such that

    \[L^{-1}d_X(x,x')-C\le d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\le Ld_X(x,x')+C\qquad(x,x'\in X).\]

    Related results and references.

  • Quasi-isometry

    Let \((X,d_X)\) and \((Y,d_Y)\) be metric spaces. A map \(f\colon X\to Y\) is a quasi-isometry if there exist constants \(L\ge1\), \(C\ge0\), and \(R\ge0\) such that

    1. for all \(x,x'\in X\),

      \[L^{-1}d_X(x,x')-C\le d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\le Ld_X(x,x')+C;\]

    2. for each \(y\in Y\), there exists \(x\in X\) with \(d_Y(y,f(x))\le R\).
  • Quasi-isomorphism

    Let \(G\) and \(H\) be equipped with bi-invariant metrics. A quasi-isomorphism is a quasi-homomorphism \(q\colon G\to H\) for which there exists a quasi-homomorphism \(r\colon H\to G\) satisfying

    \[r\circ q\sim\operatorname{id}_G,\qquad q\circ r\sim\operatorname{id}_H.\]

  • Quasimorphism

    A quasimorphism on \(G\) is a function \(q\colon G\to\mathbb R\) such that

    \[\sup_{g,h\in G}|q(gh)-q(g)-q(h)|<\infty.\]

  • Rational bijection

    A bijection between sets of words over finite alphabets is rational if its graph is a rational transduction. For free groups with specified finite bases, the graph is taken on the unique freely reduced representatives.

    Related results and references.

  • Rational representative

    Fix a finite free basis of \(F_n\). A representative \(f\in\operatorname{qAut}(F_n)\) is rational if the set of pairs \((u,v)\) of freely reduced words with \([v]=f([u])\) is a rational transduction. The subset of \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\) consisting of classes admitting such a representative is denoted by \(R_n\).

    Related results and references.

  • Rational transduction

    Let \(A\) and \(B\) be finite alphabets. A relation \(R\subseteq A^*\times B^*\) is a rational transduction if it is recognized by a finite transducer. A function is rational if its graph is a rational transduction.

    Related results and references.

  • Real character

    A real character of a topological group \(G\) is a continuous homomorphism \(\chi\colon G\to(\mathbb R,+)\). For a metric group, it is a Lipschitz real character if it is Lipschitz for the given metric.

    Related results and references.

  • Scaling sequence

    A scaling sequence is a sequence \(s=(s_j)_{j\ge1}\) of positive real numbers such that \(s_j\to\infty\).

    Related results and references.

  • Sequential transducer

    A sequential transducer is a finite transducer with one initial state, one input letter on each edge, and at most one outgoing edge with each input letter at a given state. Output labels are finite words. A fixed initial output and a final output at each accepting state may be specified; conventions allowing final outputs are also called subsequential. Such a transducer defines a partial function on input words.

    Related results and references.

  • Simultaneous quasi-isometries

    Let \(Q_n^{\mathrm w}\) be the subgroup of \(\operatorname{qAut}(F_n)\) whose elements are quasi-isometries for the ordinary word metric \(d_{\mathrm w}\). Its image in \(\operatorname{qOut}(F_n)\) is denoted by \(\overline Q_n^{\mathrm w}\).

    Related results and references.

  • Spectral gap at zero

    A nonnegative function \(\tau\colon G\to[0,\infty)\) has a spectral gap at zero if there exists \(\varepsilon>0\) such that \(\tau(G)\cap(0,\varepsilon)=\varnothing\).

    Related results and references.

  • Stable length

    Let \(\nu\) be a norm on a group \(G\). The stable length of \(g\in G\) is

    \[\tau_\nu(g)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\nu(g^k)}{k}=\inf_{k\ge1}\frac{\nu(g^k)}{k}.\]

    For the cancellation norm, write \(\tau(g)\). The term translation length is also used.

    Related results and references.

  • Torsion element

    An element \(g\) of a group is a torsion element if \(g^r=1\) for some integer \(r\ge1\). Its order is the least such integer. An element of order two is an involution.

    Related results and references.

  • Ulam metric

    Represent \(\sigma\in S_n\) by the word \(\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(n)\). The Ulam metric on \(S_n\) is

    \[d_U(\sigma,\pi)=n-\operatorname{LCS}(\sigma,\pi).\]

    It counts moves which remove one symbol and reinsert it elsewhere. Each such move is counted once.

    Related results and references.

  • Ultralimit of a bounded real sequence

    Let \(\omega\) be an ultrafilter on \(\mathbb N\), and let \((a_j)\) be a bounded real sequence. Its ultralimit is the unique real number \(a\), denoted by \(\lim_\omega a_j\), such that \(\{j:|a_j-a|<\varepsilon\}\in\omega\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\).

    Related results and references.

  • Undistorted cyclic subgroup

    Let \(G\) be a group with a left-invariant metric, and let \(g\) have infinite order. The cyclic subgroup generated by \(g\) is undistorted if the map \(\mathbb Z\to G\), \(k\mapsto g^k\), is a quasi-isometric embedding, where \(\mathbb Z\) has its usual distance.

    Related results and references.

  • Word graph

    The word graph over \(S\) has vertex set \(W(S)\). Two vertices are adjacent if one is obtained from the other by inserting or deleting one letter. Each edge has length one. The injective-word graph is the induced subgraph on injective words. In the embedding statements of this survey, “word complex” refers to this incidence-graph convention, not to the ordinary 1-skeleton of a cell complex.

    Related results and references.
